# Provenance Specification v0.1

## Status

This document is a public proposal. Version 0.x may change in response to
implementation experience. A document declares its format with
`schemaVersion: "0.1"`.

## 1. Scope

Provenance records directed relationships between external sources and project
targets. It is designed for research, design, and implementation provenance in
software projects.

The key words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **REQUIRED**, **SHOULD**, **SHOULD NOT**,
and **MAY** are interpreted as described by
[RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119).

Provenance is not:

- a license or permission to reuse third-party work;
- a replacement for copyright notices or third-party notices;
- a software bill of materials;
- a build or release attestation;
- proof that a claim is true merely because a source is present;
- a requirement to publish private prompts, conversations, or evidence.

## 2. Document model

A Provenance document MUST contain:

- `schemaVersion`: the specification version used by the document;
- `project`: the project described by the record;
- `sources`: zero or more uniquely identified external sources;
- `relationships`: zero or more uniquely identified connections from a source
  to one or more project targets.

The normative machine-readable structure is
[`schema/provenance.schema.json`](../schema/provenance.schema.json). YAML and
JSON serializations are equivalent after parsing.

Unknown information MUST be omitted or explicitly represented by an allowed
status value. Producers MUST NOT invent authors, dates, licenses, revisions, or
historical usage to satisfy the format.

## 3. Project

`project.name` is REQUIRED. A project MAY include:

- `description` — a short description;
- `repository` — the canonical repository URI;
- `version` — the version or release described by the document.

A record MAY describe a whole repository, package, application, dataset, or
other coherent unit of work.

## 4. Sources

Every source MUST have:

- `id` — a stable lowercase kebab-case identifier;
- `type` — one of the source types defined by the schema;
- `title` — a human-readable title;
- `terms` — the known status of licensing or usage terms;
- at least one retrievable `url` or `archiveUrl`, except for a personal
  communication.

A source MAY include creators, publisher, publication and access dates, archive
location, immutable revision, integrity digest, and notes.

Source identifiers SHOULD remain stable when display metadata changes. Separate
records SHOULD be used when a revision materially changes the source that
influenced the target.

### 4.1 Terms status

`terms.status` has four values:

- `verified` — terms were checked against the identified source;
- `unknown` — applicable terms were not found or not verified;
- `restricted` — known terms restrict the relevant reuse or distribution;
- `not-applicable` — no reuse or distribution governed by source terms is being
  claimed, such as a factual public standard consulted only for context.

`verified` describes recordkeeping, not a legal conclusion. A conforming tool
MUST NOT present a citation or `verified` status as permission by itself.

## 5. Relationships

Every relationship MUST have:

- `id` — a stable lowercase kebab-case identifier;
- `source` — the ID of an existing source;
- `type` — one relationship type defined below;
- `targets` — one or more affected project paths, decisions, features, releases,
  or other stable locators;
- `contribution` — a concise explanation of what the source contributed.

A relationship MAY include confidence, recording date, recorder, evidence,
notes, and extensions. Evidence SHOULD point to a reviewable commit, pull
request, issue, file, release, or other locator. Evidence MUST NOT include
credentials, signed URLs, private messages, or unnecessary personal data.

### 5.1 Relationship types

#### `consulted`

The source was considered as background. This type MUST NOT imply that its
recommendation was adopted.

#### `inspired`

The source affected the target's direction, composition, behavior, or design,
without copying code, text, or assets. A record SHOULD explain the specific
idea rather than merely say “inspired by.”

#### `adapted`

The target transforms a pattern, method, design, text, code, or other material
from the source. The record SHOULD describe both the retained idea and the
material difference.

#### `copied`

The target reproduces source material substantially or verbatim. A distribution
conformance claim requires verified terms and any required notices alongside the
shipped material.

#### `bundled`

The source material is distributed with the project. A distribution conformance
claim requires verified terms, required attribution, and required license text
in the distributed artifact.

#### `verified`

The source supplied evidence for a factual claim, behavior, test expectation,
compatibility statement, or measurement. The contribution MUST identify the
claim or expectation that was checked.

## 6. Confidence

Relationships MAY declare:

- `verified` — the relationship was checked against available evidence;
- `inferred` — the relationship is a reasoned reconstruction;
- `unknown` — the available record cannot establish confidence.

Confidence applies to the source-to-target relationship, not to the truth of all
claims within the source.

## 7. Conformance

Implementations MAY claim the following levels independently.

### 7.1 Structural conformance

A document is structurally conformant when it validates against the schema for
its declared `schemaVersion`.

### 7.2 Referential conformance

A document is referentially conformant when:

- source IDs are unique;
- relationship IDs are unique;
- every relationship references an existing source;
- every source participates in at least one relationship.

The reference CLI checks both structural and referential conformance.

### 7.3 Distribution conformance

A project MAY claim distribution conformance when every `copied` or `bundled`
relationship has verified terms, the project satisfies those terms, and all
required notices and license texts ship with the affected artifact.

Provenance tools can test record completeness, but legal compliance ultimately
requires review outside this specification.

## 8. Privacy and sensitive inputs

Committed provenance records SHOULD contain reviewed summaries, not raw capture.
Projects MUST NOT commit credentials, authentication material, signed URLs,
confidential content, entire private conversations, or unnecessary personal
data.

Personal communications SHOULD be included only with an appropriate public
summary and permission from the people identified. Private evidence MAY remain
local. Its absence does not make a public record nonconformant.

An AI system or tool MAY be recorded when the tool itself materially influenced
an outcome. Factual claims SHOULD cite the original retrievable source used to
verify them, not an unshared model response. Projects MUST NOT imply complete
capture of agent activity unless they can demonstrate it.

## 9. Extensions

Documents, sources, and relationships MAY include an `extensions` object.
Extension keys MUST use a namespaced form such as `example.org/reviewState`.
Consumers MUST preserve unknown extension values when they rewrite a document,
but MAY ignore their semantics.

An extension MUST NOT redefine a normative field or relationship type.

## 10. Interoperability

Provenance intentionally complements:

- [Citation File Format](https://citation-file-format.github.io/) for citing the
  project itself;
- [REUSE](https://reuse.software/spec/) and
  [SPDX](https://spdx.dev/use/specifications/) for licensing and software
  composition;
- [W3C PROV](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/) for general provenance data;
- build attestations for release production and verification.

The reference exporter maps sources and targets to `prov:Entity`, and maps each
relationship to a qualified `prov:Influence`. Provenance relationship types and
contribution text remain in the project namespace.

## 11. Versioning

The specification uses major and minor versions.

- Minor 0.x revisions MAY introduce breaking changes while the proposal is
  experimental.
- Version 1.0 and later MUST increment the major version for breaking changes.
- Compatible additions increment the minor version.
- A document MUST declare exactly one supported `schemaVersion`.

Schemas and specifications for published versions remain immutable. Corrections
that affect validation require a new version.
